The strongest argument against each recommendation
Against funding/basis
Funding can look structural while actually compensating you for:
	•	basis risk
	•	liquidation risk
	•	leverage demand
	•	adverse selection
	•	changing funding regimes.
A delta-neutral position isn't risk-free.
And funding is endogenous: once enough capital harvests it, the opportunity can compress.
Therefore: treat it as another empirical hypothesis, not "free money."

Against cross-sectional trading
This is probably the biggest source of hidden research fraud.
You can accidentally construct:
"the coins that survived"
rather than:
"the coins that existed."
A dynamic crypto universe can produce catastrophic survivorship bias.
Furthermore, the apparently huge nominal sample size can be mostly fake because all assets respond to the same market shock.
And recent research itself gives conflicting conclusions on crypto cross-sectional momentum depending on methodology and realistic implementation. (SSRN)
Therefore: point-in-time universe construction is non-negotiable.

Against EXP-12 regime prediction
The strongest objection is devastating:
There is one sign-flip year.
That's it.
You can invent 20 explanations for 2023.
With one observation, you cannot distinguish them.
A regime classifier trained to recognize "2023-like conditions" could simply be a sophisticated historical-description machine.
Therefore: run the experiment cheaply, but don't let it become a project.

Against changing the label
A new target can absolutely reveal economically useful information.
But this is also the easiest route to p-hacking.

Against doing nothing

If the goal is research methodology, continuing is defensible.
